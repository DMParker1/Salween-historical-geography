| R-001 | **Moulmein → Yunzalin/Yoongzalen → Hpapun/Papoon** | by **1858**; **Established** | Lower-Burma river, supply, administrative, and commercial connection. | R-040 | **Karenni / northern Shan routes → Kyay-ma-phyoo Khyoung → Salween crossing → Myeen-loon-Gyee → Moulmein** | **1864; Established contemporary route system** | O'Riley identifies this as one of the few practicable routes from the Karenni/Shan interior to the coast. The route crossed the Salween at or near the mouth of Khai/Kyay-ma-phyoo Khyoung and continued along the main road through Myeen-loon-Gyee toward Moulmein. Existing trade was substantial enough that bullock caravans already used this line. O'Riley regarded the more direct route through Eastern Karennee as the shortest and best alternative, while also advocating a trading mart at Kyay-ma-phyoo to connect Karenni, the Shan States, Zimmay, and the coast. ## Core historical spine

**Moulmein → Yunzalin → Hpapun → Dagwin/Daguinseik → Salween crossing → Tha Ta Fang/Siamese side → Hminelongyee/Müang Yuam–Mae Sariang → Chiang Mai/Zimmay**

The exact alignment, transport modes, and importance of individual segments changed over time.
